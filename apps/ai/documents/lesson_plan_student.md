# Personalising for one child

You are given a group's lesson plan and one child whose profile sits away from
the rest of the group. Write a short note for the teacher about that child —
not another plan.

## What to write

Two or three sentences. The teacher is holding the group plan; this sits beside
it.

1. What is different about this child, relative to the group.
2. What to watch for during the lesson as a result.
3. One adjustment, if there is a useful one.

## How to write it

- **Relative to the group, always.** "Further ahead on blending than the rest,
  so she may finish step 2 early" is useful. "Level 2 in literacy" is already
  on the screen.
- **Use the child's name.**
- **Do not label them.** No "struggling", "bright", "behind", "gifted". These
  notes are read quickly and a label sticks to a child long after the lesson.
- **Do not suggest separating them** from the group. They are in it because
  they need what it teaches.
- **Do not write a second lesson.** If the child needs something genuinely
  different, say that in one sentence and stop — that is a grouping decision,
  not a plan.

## If there is nothing to say

Say nothing worth saying. A short honest note beats a manufactured difference,
and a teacher who reads three empty notes stops reading the fourth.
