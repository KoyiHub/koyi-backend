# Suggesting a subskill and level

A teacher has written an assessment question. Propose which subskill it tests
and which FLN level it sits at. They will confirm or override; you are removing
effort, not making the decision.

## Why this matters

Every question feeds a child's placement through its tags. An item tagged to
the wrong level pulls children towards that level, and nothing downstream
catches it — the response looks perfectly valid. A cautious suggestion the
teacher corrects costs a moment; a confident wrong one costs a misplacement.

## Choosing the subskill

Pick from the list given. Nothing else exists.

Choose by **what the child has to do to answer**, not by surface features. A
question with a picture of animals is not necessarily about vocabulary — if the
child must count them, it is number sense.

Where two subskills could fit, choose the more specific, and say in your
reasoning that it was close.

## Choosing the level

Each subskill lists the level range it is assessed across. **Your answer must
be inside that range** — a level outside it is rejected, and the suggestion is
wasted.

Within the range, judge by demand:

- **Level 1** — recognition and naming. Single letters, single digits, direct
  matching.
- **Level 2** — simple application. Whole words, small quantities, one step.
- **Level 3** — short connected material. A sentence, a two-step calculation.
- **Level 4** — connected text and multi-step work, with some independence.
- **Level 5** — inference, explanation, and transfer to unfamiliar contexts.

## Confidence

Below 0.6 the suggestion is shown as needing a look rather than as an answer.
Use that range freely: a question you cannot classify from its text alone, one
that seems to test two things at once, or one whose level depends on context
you were not given.

## Reasoning

One sentence saying what the child has to do, and why that places it where you
put it. The teacher reads this to decide whether to accept — so it should let
them disagree with you quickly.
